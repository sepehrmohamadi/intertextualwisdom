The Jewish Share of the Land from the Nile to the Euphrates

Author: Sepehr Mohammadi

First written: Aug 2012 | Last revised: September 2026

In the name of God

Introduction

It is commonly assumed that the promise of the Nile-to-Euphrates territory in the Torah was given to the Jews, and Muslims often claim that since the Jews had the Book in hand, such a promise is recorded in it — and this is in turn counted as one of the signs of the corruption of the Old Testament. Even many Muslim intellectuals share this view.

In this article, however, I intend to examine the question on the basis of the Jews’ most authoritative document — the very Torah that exists today, whose text the Quran broadly confirms — and then to calculate the share of the Jewish people in the Nile-to-Euphrates territory; other Jewish texts are also used from time to time.

In the end, I conclude that if “the Jews” is taken to mean the descendants of Judah (a son of Jacob), their share of the Nile-to-Euphrates territory is about 0.5%, and their share of the Holy Land, Canaan (roughly today’s occupied Palestine), is about 8%.

And if “the Jews” is taken to mean the creed and religion of Moses, they have no share at all in the Nile-to-Euphrates territory — unless the followers of Abraham are also counted among the addressees of the promise, in which case the followers of all the Abrahamic religions could claim the Nile to the Euphrates!

For clarity, we will go through the details of the matter step by step, fully documented.

Note that the Bible quotations in this edition follow the English Standard Version (ESV). The Quran quotations follow The Message: A Modern Literal Translation of the Quran (Progressive Muslims Organization, 2003). This is a modern, literal English rendering whose wording stays deliberately close to the Arabic and renders the divine names in English (“God, the Most Merciful”) rather than “Allah”; being lexicon-driven and independent of later exegetical tradition, it is well suited to the comparative, text-based argument of this article, in which exact Arabic terms such as aʾimmatan and shīʿatihi matter. The translation has been released into the public domain by its copyright holder; the text quoted here is the Wikisource edition, cited as “(The Message, Q sūrah:āyah)” and listed in full in the Sources section at the end.

The texts in the Old Testament

In all, the borders of the territory promised to the offspring of Abraham and to the children of Israel are described in three places in the Old Testament.

The three passages are as follows:

Genesis 15:18–21 (ESV): 18 On that day the Lord made a covenant with Abram, saying, “To your offspring I give this land, from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates, 19 the land of the Kenites, the Kenizzites, the Kadmonites, 20 the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Rephaim, 21 the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Girgashites and the Jebusites.”

(The ESV main text reads “I give”; its footnote offers “have given” — the reading the old Persian translation follows.)

Summary of sections 1 and 2

As is clearly visible in the verses above, the promise of the Nile to the Euphrates was given to the offspring of Abraham — not to Abraham alone — and the land of Canaan, a small part of that territory, was at one point in history assigned to the children of Israel. Note also that the followers of no religion — neither the religion of Judaism, nor the religion of Moses, nor any other — are named anywhere in the verses above.

The fundamental error of the Jews is their claim that these lands were inherited by them alone! That is, all the land promised to the children of Abraham passed as inheritance to Isaac and to Jacob, and all the land promised to the children of Israel passed as inheritance only to the tribe of Judah (the Jews)! This claim is utterly baseless, and no verse of the Torah supports it! If the Jewish premise were correct — that Jacob, by blessing Joseph, gave away his entire share to the tribe of Ephraim — then once again the Jews would have no share of the Nile to the Euphrates!

3. The children of Abraham

It is customary to take Ishmael and Isaac as the two sons of Abraham, and the controversy over which of them was the intended sacrifice (al-dhabīḥ, “the one to be sacrificed”) — the Israelite prophets descending from Isaac and the Prophet of Islam from Ishmael — has kept the two at the center of attention. The Torah, however, indicates that Abraham had at least eight sons: Ishmael and Isaac, and six more sons by his third wife, whom he married after Sarah’s death and who was named Keturah:

Genesis 25:1–2 (ESV): 1 Abraham took another wife, whose name was Keturah. 2 She bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.

Someone might say these were not sons and would not have inherited by the custom of that age; but that they were sons is stated in the study note on this chapter (rendered from the Persian “Bible Guide”):

Bible Guide, Genesis 25: Keturah bore six sons to Abraham, from whom the Midianites came.

And the names of daughters were normally of so little importance that they were not recorded — besides which, it can usually be inferred from the Hebrew names themselves, which, like Arabic, distinguish masculine from feminine.

According to the Torah’s accounts, Abraham gave gifts to Ishmael and his brothers, but left his entire estate to Isaac:

Genesis 25:5–6 (ESV): 5 Abraham gave all he had to Isaac. 6 But to the sons of his concubines Abraham gave gifts, and while he was still living he sent them away from his son Isaac, eastward to the east country.

Even if this is granted, the Nile-to-Euphrates promise was not part of Abraham’s estate, to be inherited by one of his sons alone! It was a promise to all the offspring of Abraham; and an added emphasis records the important fact that Ishmael is part of the offspring of Abraham:

Genesis 21:12–13 (ESV): 12 …God said to Abraham, “Be not displeased because of the boy and because of your slave woman. Whatever Sarah says to you, do as she tells you, for through Isaac shall your offspring be named. 13 And I will make a nation of the son of the slave woman also, because he is your offspring.”

There is a subtlety in these verses that I find quite remarkable: they emphasize precisely the point this article argues! They say that Abraham’s offspring will be named through Isaac — yet Ishmael too is part of Abraham’s offspring. Just as the present-day claimants to the Bible take Abraham’s offspring to be through Isaac, the fact remains that Ishmael is part of Abraham’s offspring.

An objection answered: was God’s covenant established with Isaac alone?

It may be objected that in Genesis 17 God, while announcing the birth of Isaac to Sarah, expressly affirms the permanence and eternity of His covenant with Isaac and his descendants, promising Ishmael only blessing, multiplication, and the rise of a great nation: “But I will establish my covenant with Isaac” (Genesis 17:19–21). Likewise, in Genesis 21:12–13 the promised offspring is expressly named through Isaac. It would seem, then, that the divine covenant given in chapter 15 to all of Abraham’s offspring was in later chapters restricted to Isaac and his line (something like abrogation or specification).

The answer is that we are in fact dealing with two distinct covenants, not one:

  1. The land covenant (Nile to Euphrates), given in Genesis 15 to the “offspring of Abraham” in general and never restricted to Isaac; Ishmael remains part of Abraham’s offspring (Genesis 21:13). The sign of this covenant is circumcision, established in Genesis 17 for every male of Abraham’s house — even the bought slave, from “any foreigner who is not of your offspring”: “So shall my covenant be in your flesh an everlasting covenant.” That is, the Torah itself testifies that this covenant, beyond Isaac and Jacob, embraced all the children of Abraham.

  2. The covenant of imamate — a covenant made with individuals and, as the text expressly says, established with Isaac, passing after him to Jacob. The Quran confirms the same:

وَوَهَبْنَا لَهُ إِسْحَاقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَجَعَلْنَاهُمْ أَئِمَّةً (Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:73)

Translation: “And We made them leaders who guide by Our command, and We inspired them to do good work and hold the contact-method and contribute towards betterment, and they were in service to Us.” (The Message, Q 21:73 — the Arabic aʾimmatan means “imāms”)

The covenant of imamate’s arrival with Isaac does not mean Ishmael left the offspring of Abraham or the general land covenant; it means only that the imam after Abraham was Isaac, and after Isaac, Jacob.

Moreover, this covenant of imamate did not remain among the children of Israel; it was lost and transferred: Zechariah worried about who would succeed him, and God granted him John — and John was killed! And Jesus was crucified. So God removed the covenant of imamate from the children of Isaac and gave it to the children of Ishmael, and the Prophet of Islam was raised. This too is in the Gospel:

Matthew 21:42–44 (ESV): 42 Jesus said to them, “Have you never read in the Scriptures: ‘The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; this was the Lord’s doing, and it is marvelous in our eyes’? 43 Therefore I tell you, the kingdom of God will be taken away from you and given to a people producing its fruits. 44 And the one who falls on this stone will be broken to pieces; and when it falls on anyone, it will crush him.”

The stone the builders rejected was Hagar and Ishmael — but it came about that the same stone became the cornerstone! And the kingdom of God on earth was taken from the children of Israel and reached the children of Ishmael.

The nature of the covenant and the sign of circumcision

What exactly the “covenant” itself was is a matter of debate. What is clear is that a covenant repeated and emphasized so heavily throughout Genesis 17 must not be simplistically reduced to a hygienic procedure on male infants; circumcision is only the sign of the covenant: “You shall be circumcised in the flesh of your foreskins, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and you” (Genesis 17:11).

The content of the covenant, most plausibly, is the seven commandments of Noah — “the way of the Lord” — for the Torah says God chose Abraham to command his children and his household to keep the way of the Lord by doing righteousness and justice:

Genesis 18:19 (ESV): 19 For I have chosen him, that he may command his children and his household after him to keep the way of the Lord by doing righteousness and justice, so that the Lord may bring to Abraham what he has promised him.

So keeping the way of the Lord — which at that time was the covenant of Noah — was Abraham’s bequest to his children, and the Noble Quran counts Abraham among the sect (Shīʿa) of Noah:

وَإِنَّ مِن شِیعَتِهِ لَإِبْرَاهِیمَ (Aṣ-Ṣāffāt 37:83)

Translation: “And from among his descendants was Abraham.” (The Message, Q 37:83 — the Arabic min shīʿatihi means “of his sect/followers”)

4. The Ishmaelites and the Arabs

The word “Ishmaelites” appears in many passages of the Torah, and it is generally assumed that they are the children of Ishmael; but the Torah gives no precise definition, and biblical commentators believe that “Ishmaelites” and “Midianites” (the children of Ishmael’s brother Midian) are at times used interchangeably. The Book of Jubilees — known in Persian as Panjāh-hā (“the Fifties”), a book the Ethiopian Church counts among its canon while others do not, and of which a Hebrew copy dating to about 100 BC (that is, older than the extant Greek and Hebrew manuscripts of the Bible, which date from the third to the seventh centuries CE) was discovered among the Dead Sea Scrolls — states:

Book of Jubilees (20:13):

And Ishmael and his sons, and the sons of Keturah and their sons, went together and dwelt from Paran to the entering in of Babylon in all the land which is towards the East facing the desert. And these mingled with each other, and their name was called Arabs, and Ishmaelites.

(R. H. Charles translation, public domain)

In the passage above, the Ishmaelites are expressly defined as the sons of Ishmael together with the sons of his six brothers by Keturah, and the term “Arabs” is applied to them.

This matters because the Jews commonly deny any connection between Ishmael and the Ishmaelites and the Arabs; and if Abraham’s six sons were alongside Ishmael, the Arab share of the Nile-to-Euphrates territory is multiplied sevenfold! — a disaster for the current claimants of the “promised land”!

5. The children of Isaac and the children of Israel

Isaac had two sons, Esau and Jacob, who were twins, and Esau was the firstborn; Jacob, however, acquired the birthright from Esau in a bargain. In any case, Esau went to Edom and Jacob remained in Canaan. Isaac likewise, according to Genesis 27, bequeathed all he had to Jacob.

Jacob — that is, Israel — had twelve children, named in the Torah as follows:

Genesis 35:22–26 (ESV): 22 …Now the sons of Jacob were twelve. 23 The sons of Leah: Reuben (Jacob’s firstborn), Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun. 24 The sons of Rachel: Joseph and Benjamin. 25 The sons of Bilhah, Rachel’s servant: Dan and Naphtali. 26 The sons of Zilpah, Leah’s servant: Gad and Asher. These were the sons of Jacob who were born to him in Paddan-aram.

Jacob, further, according to Genesis 48 blessed the sons of Joseph — especially the younger, Ephraim — above all his children and grandchildren. That is, just as Abraham willed to Isaac and Isaac to Jacob, Jacob willed to Joseph and his sons (Ephraim and Manasseh).

6. Who is a Jew?

But who is a Jew? There are various views; but if we consult the Bible itself (the Old and New Testaments), the answer is clear: in the Bible, “Jew” is never a purely religious term; it denotes the inhabitants of Judea, whose core was the two tribes Judah and Benjamin (together with the Levites and remnants of the other tribes).

6-1. Old Testament evidence

The word “Jew” derives from “Judah” (and the land of Judea), and it first appears — after the secession of the northern kingdom (Israel) from the southern kingdom (Judah) — in 2 Kings:

2 Kings 16:6 (ESV): 6 At that time Rezin the king of Syria recovered Elath for Syria and drove the men of Judah from Elath, and the Edomites came to Elath, where they dwell to this day.

Note that “the men of Judah” here is the Hebrew yehūdīm — the very word rendered “Jews” elsewhere — and it stands exactly opposite “Israel”: the inhabitants of the southern kingdom. (The old Persian translation, following another textual reading, has “the Jews … the Arameans.”)

The composition of that kingdom is stated expressly in 2 Chronicles:

2 Chronicles 11:1, 12, 16 (ESV): 1 When Rehoboam came to Jerusalem, he assembled the house of Judah and Benjamin, 180,000 chosen warriors… 12 And he put shields and spears in all the cities and made them very strong. So he held Judah and Benjamin. … 16 And those who had set their hearts to seek the Lord God of Israel came after them from all the tribes of Israel to Jerusalem to sacrifice to the Lord, the God of their fathers.

That is, the kingdom of Judah rested on the two tribes of Judah and Benjamin, and the Levites and whoever from the other tribes joined Judea were counted among them.

After the Babylonian captivity, the returnees are described with the same composition:

Ezra 1:5 (ESV): 5 Then rose up the heads of the fathers’ houses of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests and the Levites, everyone whose spirit God had stirred to go up to rebuild the house of the Lord that is in Jerusalem.

Ezra 4:1–2 (ESV): 1 Now when the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard that the returned exiles were building a temple to the Lord, the God of Israel, 2 they approached Zerubbabel and the heads of fathers’ houses and said to them, “Let us build with you, for we worship your God as you do…”

The key witness, however, is the Book of Esther, in which Mordecai, though of the tribe of Benjamin, is called “a Jew”:

Esther 2:5 (ESV): 5 Now there was a Jew in Susa the citadel whose name was Mordecai, the son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite,

This verse shows that being a “Jew” in the Bible does not mean merely being a “son of Judah”; it means belonging to the people and land of Judea, of which the Benjaminites were also part. The same sense appears in Nehemiah 1:2, where the survivors of the exile are the inhabitants of the province of Judea.

6-2. New Testament evidence

In the New Testament too, the Greek word Ioudaios (Ἰουδαῖος) carries exactly this meaning: a Judean.

  • In Luke 1:5 Zechariah and Elizabeth are introduced as residents of Judea: “In the days of Herod, king of Judea, there was a priest named Zechariah, of the division of Abijah.”
  • In Matthew 3:5 the people of Jerusalem and “all Judea” come out to John: “Then Jerusalem and all Judea and all the region about the Jordan were going out to him,” and in John 1:19 the same people are called “the Jews”: “the Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, ‘Who are you?’”
  • In John 4:9 the Samaritan woman says: “How is it that you, a Jew, ask for a drink from me, a woman of Samaria?” and in John 4:22 Jesus says: “You worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, for salvation is from the Jews.” — that is, from Judea and the heritage of the southern kingdom.
  • In Acts 10:28 Peter says: “You yourselves know how unlawful it is for a Jew to associate with or to visit anyone of another nation…” — a geographic-ethnic distinction between a Judean and a non-Judean.

Another important witness is the Apostle Paul, of the tribe of Benjamin (Romans 11:1: “I myself am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, a member of the tribe of Benjamin”; Philippians 3:5: “a Hebrew of Hebrews”), and yet in Acts 21:39 and 22:3 he calls himself “a Jew”: “I am a Jew, from Tarsus in Cilicia…”; “I am a Jew, born in Tarsus in Cilicia…”. That is, in the New Testament there is no contradiction between being a Benjaminite and being a Jew, for both tribes lived in Judea.

Josephus, the first-century Jewish historian, likewise confirms in Against Apion that after the return from the Babylonian captivity the Jewish nation consisted of the three tribes of Judah, Benjamin, and Levi, and that the name “Jew” derives from Judah (Judea).

6-3. Summary: “Jew” as a geographic-genealogical term

Thus throughout the Bible, “Jew” is a geographic-genealogical term: the inhabitants of Judea, who were mostly of the descendants of Judah and Benjamin. Never in the Bible does “Jew” mean “follower of the religion of Moses” or “any monotheist”; those broader definitions are the product of later centuries. The ten tribes of the children of Israel (the northern kingdom) were lost to history and are heard of no more!

Hence, if only the sons of Judah are counted as Jews, one-twelfth of the children of Israel are Jews; and if the biblical definition — the inhabitants of the kingdom of Judah (Judah and Benjamin) — is the basis, one-sixth of the children of Israel are Jews.

But in later centuries the great controversy Who is a Jew? arose! The greatest disputes exist over this! The Talmud counts every monotheist a Jew; some count the child of a Jewish mother a Jew; some the child of a Jewish father; some a convert to the religion of Moses — whereas, as we saw, in the New Testament the inhabitants of Judea were the ones called Jews. From the standpoint of the State of Israel, the children and grandchildren of any Jew and of any convert to Judaism have the right of residence in Israel (Law of Return, paragraph 4A), though they are not counted as Jews until they formally enter the Jewish religion.

But the interesting point is this: if the biblical definition is the standard, the Jews are at most one-sixth of the children of Israel; and under any of these definitions of “Jew,” the Jews will not receive a large share of the Nile-to-Euphrates territory!

Another interesting point: recently, with the identification of the DNA of Aaron, it has become possible to distinguish the children of Israel from non-children of Israel, and this has created great problems for the State of Israel! For by DNA, most of the inhabitants of the State of Israel are not children of Israel — that is, not descendants of Jacob!!!

7. Who are the Ashkenazim?

But who are the inhabitants of the State of Israel today? A large part of the Jews of Israel and of the founders of that country are Ashkenazim — Jews who migrated from Central and Eastern Europe to Palestine and settled there from the 1880s onward, and especially after the founding of the State of Israel.

Statistically, according to a 2016 report of the Pew Research Center, about 45 percent of the Jews of Israel identify as Ashkenazi, while about 48 percent identify as Sephardi or Mizrahi (the Jews of Asia, Africa, and the Middle East, including the Jews of Iran). The Ashkenazim make up roughly 35–36 percent of the total population of the State of Israel (Arabs included). Globally, however, estimates suggest that about 75 percent of the Jews of the world are Ashkenazi.

Ashkenaz’s lineage in the Torah

Now we turn to the Torah to see how Ashkenaz’s lineage appears in the Bible. In the Table of Nations of Genesis 10, Ashkenaz is introduced as a son of Gomer, and Gomer as a son of Japheth son of Noah:

Genesis 10:1–3 (ESV): 1 These are the generations of the sons of Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth. Sons were born to them after the flood. 2 The sons of Japheth: Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech, and Tiras. 3 The sons of Gomer: Ashkenaz, Riphath, and Togarmah.

So by the text of the Torah, Ashkenaz descends from Japheth son of Noah, not from Shem son of Noah! Whereas the promise of the Nile to the Euphrates was given to the offspring of Abraham, and Abraham descends from Shem (Genesis 11:10–26 traces Abraham’s lineage through Arpachshad to Shem son of Noah). The Ashkenazim, therefore, by the very Torah text they themselves invoke, cannot be parties to the promise at all: the promise lies in the line of Shem and Abraham, and they are of the line of Japheth!

Who are Gog and Magog?

Magog, Ashkenaz’s uncle (Genesis 10:2), also has an interesting fate in the Bible. Note that Magog must not be confused with Gog: “Gog” in 1 Chronicles 5:4 appears among the descendants of Joel, of the line of Reuben son of Israel:

1 Chronicles 5:3–4 (ESV): 3 The sons of Reuben, the firstborn of Israel: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi. 4 The sons of Joel: Shemaiah his son, Gog his son, Shimei his son,

And the Book of Ezekiel (chapters 38 and 39) foresees the attack of Gog and Magog on the land of Israel in the last days — a land where all the nations dwell in security:

Ezekiel 38:8, 11, 16 (ESV): 8 …In the latter years you will go against the land that is restored from war, the land whose people were gathered from many peoples upon the mountains of Israel, which had been a continual waste. Its people were brought out from the peoples and now dwell securely, all of them. … 11 and say, ‘I will go up against the land of unwalled villages. Ezekiel 39:17–19 (ESV): 17 “As for you, son of man, thus says the Lord God: Speak to the birds of every sort and to all beasts of the field: ‘Assemble and come, gather from all around to the sacrificial feast that I am preparing for you, a great sacrificial feast on the mountains of Israel, and you shall eat flesh and drink blood. 18 You shall eat the flesh of the mighty, and drink the blood of the princes of the earth… 19 And you shall eat fat till you are filled, and drink blood till you are drunk, at the sacrificial feast that I am preparing for you.’”

So by the Bible, the Ashkenazim — of the line of Japheth and not of Shem — have no share in the promise of the Nile to the Euphrates; and on the other side, Gog and Magog (Arabic Yājūj wa-Mājūj), who in the last days seize the land of Israel by force and like a cloud, have no fate but the great slaughter in the mountains of Israel! The only party to the promise is the offspring of Abraham from the line of Shem.

8. Conclusion

So far we have set out the verses concerning the Nile-to-Euphrates territory promised to the children of Abraham; it became clear that this promise was not made to any particular person but to all the children of Abraham; it became clear that Abraham had eight sons, of whom one was Isaac, while Ishmael and his six brothers by Keturah formed the Ishmaelites and the Arabs. It also became clear in section 3 that the restriction of the covenant to Isaac in Genesis 17 concerns the covenant of imamate, not the land covenant of the Nile to the Euphrates, whose sign is circumcision and which embraces all the children of Abraham.

Isaac had two sons, and Jacob twelve; so if — hypothetically, and on the basis of the Torah’s own verses — we divide the Nile-to-Euphrates territory equally among the sons of Abraham, seven parts of eight go to the Arabs: that is, 87.5% of it!

But of the remaining share of the territory, half — that is, 6.25% — goes to all the children of Israel; and under the different definitions of “Jew,” the Jewish share of the Nile-to-Euphrates territory would be:

  • If, with the Talmud, every monotheist — or every convert to the religion of Moses — is a Jew, the Jews, by virtue of being Jews, can claim no share of the Nile to the Euphrates!
  • If only the sons of Judah are counted Jews, one-twelfth of the children of Israel’s share reaches them — that is, 0.5% of the Nile-to-Euphrates territory goes to the Jews!
  • If, as in the New Testament, the two tribes of the kingdom of Judah are counted Jews, at most 1% of the Nile-to-Euphrates territory goes to the Jews!

If we grant that the promise of the land of Canaan to the children of Israel is definitive and final, then of the land of Canaan (the present State of Israel) the Jews would receive:

  • If, with the Talmud, every monotheist — or every convert to the religion of Moses — is a Jew, the Jews, by virtue of being Jews, can claim no share of present-day Israel!
  • If only the sons of Judah are counted Jews, 8.3% of the present State of Israel goes to the Jews.
  • If the two tribes of the kingdom of Judea are counted Jews, at most 16.6% of the present State of Israel goes to the Jews.

All this, of course, assumes that the Jewish claimants can prove their relation to Judah and Jacob! And as we saw in the previous section, the principal claimants — the Ashkenazi Jews — are, by the very text of the Torah, of the line of Japheth son of Noah and not of Shem: their lineage reaches not to Judah and Jacob, nor even to Abraham; while Gog and Magog, who according to Ezekiel’s prophecy will occupy the land of Israel by force and like a cloud in the last days, have no fate but the great slaughter in the mountains of Israel (Ezekiel 39). So neither the Ashkenazim nor Gog and Magog will have any share of the Nile to the Euphrates! And as I said before, by DNA testing too, most of the present inhabitants of the State of Israel are not children of Israel or descendants of Jacob at all!!!
